\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

We use a subset of the FNSPID dataset~\citep{dong2024fnspid} covering two Nasdaq tickers, MSFT and
TSLA, with full article text, headline, and timestamp for each article, at a fixed rate of 4
articles/day per ticker.
For each ticker we define a common in-sample window of 200 calendar days, from 2 May 2022 to 18
November 2022, used for the ontology-evolution sweep and the temporal train/validation split defined
in Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}, and hold out a subsequent 100-day out-of-sample window, from 19
November 2022 to 26 February 2023, that is not seen during ontology search and is reserved for final
evaluation of the selected ontology.
The window starts at 2 May 2022, where both tickers reach consistently dense article volume; earlier
months are excluded due to sparse coverage.
Figure~\ref{fig:windows} shows adjusted close price and daily article volume for each ticker over
the full history, with the in-sample and out-of-sample windows overlaid.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We compare against two reference conditions, both targeted by H\ref{hyp:accuracy}.
The text-only baseline, described in Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}, isolates the
contribution of graph structure by replacing KG-derived features with mean-pooled article text
embeddings under an identical day set and temporal split.
The static-ontology baseline is an independently constructed graph, built by the same KG Builder
Agent and LLM extraction pipeline as the task-aware candidates (Section~\ref{sec:construction})
using the default construction method, but never revised by the Ontology Evolution Agent in
response to the downstream feedback signal; this isolates the contribution of the feedback loop
itself from that of KG construction in general.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}

The pipeline separates two LLM roles that differ in required capacity and call volume.

\paragraph{Extraction SLM}
The KG Builder Agent (Section~\ref{sec:construction}) issues one extraction call per article and
is the highest-volume LLM consumer in the loop.
We serve it with an AWQ-quantized small language model, \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ}, through
a self-hosted vLLM server exposing an OpenAI-compatible API on a single GPU node (8192-token
context), trading model capacity for the throughput needed to run many candidates in parallel
under the sweep budget.

\paragraph{Ontology Evolution LLM}
The Ontology Evolution Agent (Section~\ref{sec:evolution}) issues one proposal call per step and
reasons over the aggregated feedback signal from all candidates at that step, a lower-volume,
higher-reasoning task.
We serve this role with \texttt{qwen.qwen3-32b-v1:0} via AWS Bedrock.

\paragraph{Embeddings}
Article and node text embeddings (feature construction and the text-only baseline) use
\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, served through Hugging Face Text Embeddings
Inference (TEI) on a CPU node.

\subsection{Sweep Configuration}
\label{sec:sweep-config}

Rather than a full grid search, configurations are chosen so that each dimension in
Table~\ref{tab:sweep-dims} can be compared on equal footing.
When the levels of one dimension are compared --- three evolution steps against five and seven, say
--- the remaining dimensions are distributed identically across those groups, so a difference
between the groups reflects the dimension being varied rather than an uneven mix of everything else.
This is the standard balanced factorial, or orthogonal array, strategy~\citep{lin2025orthogonalarrays},
which buys that comparability at a small fraction of the runs a full grid over the same levels would
demand; such designs have proven effective for exploring machine-learning hyperparameter spaces in
particular~\citep{shi2023hpdesigns}.
The design is crossed with two tickers, MSFT and TSLA.
Candidates per step is fixed at 3, articles per day at 4, and feature mode at \texttt{hybrid}
(Section~\ref{sec:features}) throughout.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Dimension & Levels \\
\midrule
Evolution steps & 3, 5, 7 \\
Lookback window (days) & 3, 8, 10, 20 \\
Chain hops (min = max) & 3, 5, 6 \\
Evolution prompt & \texttt{default}, \texttt{fundamental}, \texttt{event-driven}, \texttt{macro-context} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Dimensions crossed in the balanced factorial sweep, further crossed with ticker
(MSFT, TSLA).}
\label{tab:sweep-dims}
\end{table}

The four evolution-prompt variants bias the Ontology Evolution Agent toward different semantic
categories when proposing new node and relationship types.
Each ticker receives its own 36-configuration design over the remaining four dimensions, for 72
configurations in total (36 MSFT, 36 TSLA).
The two tickers are analysed separately rather than pooled: they are different prediction problems,
of differing difficulty, so their AUC values are not meaningfully comparable on the same scale.
The two per-ticker designs share the same structure, so the effect of a given dimension can still be
read off in each and the two readings set side by side.
Effects are read from group means: the configurations sharing a given level of one dimension are
averaged and compared against those sharing each other level.
Evolution steps and chain hops --- the two dimensions of primary interest --- are balanced against
every other dimension, so these comparisons are clean.
Lookback and evolution prompt are balanced against everything except each other, where a few joint
combinations go uncovered, so comparisons along those two dimensions are read with more caution.

\subsection{Infrastructure}
\label{sec:infrastructure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/infrastructure.png}
  \caption{Experimental infrastructure.}
  \label{fig:infrastructure}
\end{figure}

The ontology-evolution sweep is run on the cloud pipeline shown in Figure~\ref{fig:infrastructure}:
a push to the \texttt{finance-kg-builder} repository triggers a GitHub Actions workflow that
provisions the Amazon EKS cluster via Terraform, builds and pushes the orchestrator image. A separate 
dispatch workflow launches the sweep as parallel Kubernetes jobs, each pairing a KG Builder job pod with a Neo4j
sidecar, a CPU node group serving embeddings, and a GPU node group serving the extraction SLM
(Section~\ref{sec:models}); run metrics are logged to DagsHub MLflow, and ontology-proposal calls
are routed to AWS Bedrock.
