\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

We use a subset of the FNSPID dataset~\citep{dong2024fnspid} covering three Nasdaq tickers ---
NVDA, MSFT, and TSLA --- comprising 26{,}165 news articles (8{,}716, 8{,}737, and 8{,}712
articles respectively) with full article text, headline, and timestamp.
For each ticker we define a common in-sample window from 2 May 2022 to 16 August 2023 (338
trading days), used for the ontology-evolution sweep and the temporal train/validation split defined in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}, and hold out a subsequent 4-month out-of-sample window from 16
August 2023 to 16 December 2023 (88 trading days) that is not seen during ontology search and is
reserved for final evaluation of the selected ontology.
The window starts at TSLA's first coverage date (2 May 2022), where all three tickers reach
consistently dense article volume; earlier months are excluded due to sparse MSFT and TSLA
coverage (fewer than 10 articles/day at the 25th percentile).
Figure~\ref{fig:windows} shows adjusted close price and daily article volume for each ticker over
the full history, with the in-sample and out-of-sample windows overlaid.
NVDA is retained here for descriptive completeness but excluded from the primary sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:sweep-config}).

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We compare against two reference conditions, both targeted by H\ref{hyp:accuracy}.
The text-only baseline, described in Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}, isolates the
contribution of graph structure by replacing KG-derived features with mean-pooled article text
embeddings under an identical day set and temporal split.
The static-ontology baseline is an independently constructed graph, built by the same KG Builder
Agent and LLM extraction pipeline as the task-aware candidates (Section~\ref{sec:construction})
using the default construction method, but never revised by the Ontology Evolution Agent in
response to the downstream feedback signal; this isolates the contribution of the feedback loop
itself from that of KG construction in general.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}

The pipeline separates two LLM roles that differ in required capacity and call volume.

\paragraph{Extraction SLM}
The KG Builder Agent (Section~\ref{sec:construction}) issues one extraction call per article and
is the highest-volume LLM consumer in the loop.
We serve it with an AWQ-quantized small language model, \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ}, through
a self-hosted vLLM server exposing an OpenAI-compatible API on a single GPU node (8192-token
context), trading model capacity for the throughput needed to run many candidates in parallel
under the sweep budget.

\paragraph{Ontology Evolution LLM}
The Ontology Evolution Agent (Section~\ref{sec:evolution}) issues one proposal call per step and
reasons over the aggregated feedback signal from all candidates at that step, a lower-volume,
higher-reasoning task.
We serve this role with \texttt{qwen.qwen3-32b-v1:0} via AWS Bedrock rather than the local vLLM
deployment.

\paragraph{Embeddings}
Article and node text embeddings (feature construction and the text-only baseline) use
\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, served through Hugging Face Text Embeddings
Inference (TEI) on a CPU node.

\subsection{Sweep Configuration}
\label{sec:sweep-config}

Rather than a full grid search, the sweep is organized as single-dimension ablations around one
reference configuration (3 evolution steps, 3-day lookback, chain hops fixed at 5, 2 candidates
per step, 5 articles/day, hybrid feature mode of Section~\ref{sec:features}): each group varies
exactly one dimension while holding all others at their reference value, so that per-dimension
effects are not confounded by simultaneous changes elsewhere.
Table~\ref{tab:sweep-dims} lists the swept dimensions.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Dimension & Values swept & Reference \\
\midrule
Evolution steps & 0, 3, 4, 5 & 3 \\
Lookback window (days) & 1, 3, 5, 7 & 3 \\
Chain hops (min = max) & 4, 5, 6 & 5 \\
Candidates per step & 1, 2, 3 & 2 \\
Articles per day & 3, 5, 10, 20 & 5 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Dimensions ablated in the sweep and the reference value each is held at when another
dimension is varied.}
\label{tab:sweep-dims}
\end{table}

Every configuration in every group is further crossed with four evolution-prompt variants
(\texttt{default}, \texttt{fundamental}, \texttt{event-driven}, \texttt{macro-context}), which
bias the Ontology Evolution Agent toward different semantic categories when proposing new node
and relationship types, and with two tickers, MSFT and TSLA.
NVDA is excluded from the primary sweep: over the training window it undergoes a near-4$\times$
rally driven by the AI sector narrative, so next-day direction is dominated by macro demand rather
than article-level graph signal, making the prediction task either trivially easy or
uninformative.
Combined, this design totals 138 jobs: 32 for the lookback sweep, 26 for the evolution-depth sweep
(2 no-evolution jobs plus 24 evolved), 24 for the chain-hop sweep, 24 for the candidate-breadth
sweep, and 32 for the articles-per-day sweep.

\subsection{Infrastructure}
\label{sec:infrastructure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/infrastructure.png}
  \caption{Experimental infrastructure.}
  \label{fig:infrastructure}
\end{figure}

The ontology-evolution sweep is run on the cloud pipeline shown in Figure~\ref{fig:infrastructure}:
a push to the \texttt{finance-kg-builder} repository triggers a GitHub Actions workflow that
provisions the Amazon EKS cluster via Terraform, builds and pushes the orchestrator image. A separate 
dispatch workflow launches the sweep as parallel Kubernetes jobs, each pairing a KG Builder job pod with a Neo4j
sidecar, a CPU node group serving embeddings, and a GPU node group serving the extraction SLM
(Section~\ref{sec:models}); run metrics are logged to DagsHub MLflow, and ontology-proposal calls
are routed to AWS Bedrock.
