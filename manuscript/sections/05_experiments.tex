\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

We use a subset of the FNSPID dataset~\citep{dong2024fnspid} covering three Nasdaq tickers ---
NVDA, MSFT, and TSLA --- comprising 26{,}165 news articles (8{,}716, 8{,}737, and 8{,}712
articles respectively) with full article text, headline, and timestamp.
For each ticker we define a common in-sample window from 2 May 2022 to 16 August 2023 (338
trading days), used for the ontology-evolution sweep and the temporal train/validation split of
Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}, and hold out a subsequent 4-month out-of-sample window from 16
August 2023 to 16 December 2023 (88 trading days) that is not seen during ontology search and is
reserved for final evaluation of the selected ontology.
News coverage is sparse in early 2022 for MSFT and TSLA (fewer than 10 articles/day in the 25th
percentile), which motivates aligning the common window on TSLA's first coverage date rather than
on the full price history.
Figure~\ref{fig:windows} shows adjusted close price and daily article volume for each ticker over
the full history, with the in-sample and out-of-sample windows overlaid.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We compare against two reference conditions.
The \emph{text-only baseline} of Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol} isolates the contribution of
graph structure by replacing KG-derived features with mean-pooled article text embeddings under
an identical day set and temporal split.
The \emph{static-ontology baseline} targeted by H\ref{hyp:accuracy} freezes the schema at the seed
ontology of Section~\ref{sec:ontology-repr} for the full step budget, so that no evolution takes
place; this isolates the contribution of the feedback loop itself from that of KG construction in
general.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}

The pipeline separates two LLM roles that differ in required capacity and call volume.

\paragraph{Extraction SLM}
The KG Builder Agent (Section~\ref{sec:construction}) issues one extraction call per article and
is the highest-volume LLM consumer in the loop.
We serve it with an AWQ-quantized small language model, \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ}, through
a self-hosted vLLM server exposing an OpenAI-compatible API on a single GPU node (8192-token
context), trading model capacity for the throughput needed to run many candidates in parallel
under the sweep budget.

\paragraph{Ontology Evolution LLM}
The Ontology Evolution Agent (Section~\ref{sec:evolution}) issues one proposal call per step and
reasons over the aggregated feedback signal from all candidates at that step, a lower-volume,
higher-reasoning task.
We serve this role with \texttt{qwen.qwen3-32b-v1:0} via AWS Bedrock rather than the local vLLM
deployment.

\paragraph{Embeddings}
Article and node text embeddings (feature construction and the text-only baseline) use
\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, served through Hugging Face Text Embeddings
Inference (TEI) on a CPU node.

\subsection{Sweep Configuration}
\label{sec:sweep-config}

Each run of the ontology-evolution loop is configured by a target ticker, a number of evolution
steps, and a number of candidate ontologies proposed per step; the sweep reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results} runs all three tickers independently under matched step and candidate
budgets so that results are comparable across tickers.

\subsection{Infrastructure}
\label{sec:infrastructure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/infrastructure.png}
  \caption{Experimental infrastructure.}
  \label{fig:infrastructure}
\end{figure}

The ontology-evolution sweep is run on the cloud pipeline shown in Figure~\ref{fig:infrastructure}:
a push to the \texttt{finance-kg-builder} repository triggers a GitHub Actions workflow that
provisions the Amazon EKS cluster via Terraform, builds and pushes the orchestrator image, and
launches the sweep as parallel Kubernetes jobs, each pairing a KG Builder job pod with a Neo4j
sidecar, a CPU node group serving embeddings, and a GPU node group serving the extraction SLM
(Section~\ref{sec:models}); run metrics are logged to DagsHub MLflow, and ontology-proposal calls
are routed to AWS Bedrock.
